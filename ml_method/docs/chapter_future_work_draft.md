## Future Directions

DRAFT for the thesis. Prose style follows the thesis convention of no semicolons, no
non-mathematical parentheses, and no em-dashes. The directions are ordered by how directly they
follow from the results and the diagnostic analysis.

PlaceNet is under active development. The directions below follow from the results and the
diagnostic analysis, and the first of them addresses the backbone.

The first direction is removing the dependence on a single backbone, which the diagnostic analysis
identifies as the only lever able to lower the in-distribution μ-distance materially. Three
approaches to it are open.

The most direct is to phase the subgenomes of the polyploids, so that ASTRAL is given single-copy
lineages and the backbone is no longer a compromise between the two parental positions of every
polyploid. This removes the cause rather than its effect, but phasing acts before the backbone is
built, so it constitutes a different method rather than a modification of this one.

The second approach keeps the backbone but stops relying on one of them. A set of candidate
backbones can be built, for instance by bootstrapping the gene trees or by sampling among the
near-optimal ASTRAL trees, and PlaceNet run on each to give one reconstruction per backbone. Two
rules for choosing among the resulting MUL-trees are worth testing. One selects the centroid, the
MUL-tree with the smallest total distance to all the others, taking agreement among the backbones as
evidence. The other scores each reconstruction by the confidence of the model in it, summing the
detection probabilities of the accepted events together with the partner probabilities assigned to
their chosen parental lineages, and selects the highest. Neither rule is of use unless the quantity
it ranks on predicts accuracy, so both first require an analysis on simulated data of how strongly
the selection criterion correlates with the μ-distance of each candidate reconstruction to the true
network. That analysis also establishes whether the confidence of the model is calibrated at the
level of a whole reconstruction, which nothing reported here tests.

The third approach is to measure what the backbone costs on the benchmark networks rather than only
on the simulated ones. The diagnostic analysis computed its floor and its ceiling on networks drawn
from the training simulator, where the true species tree is known by construction. A comparable true
backbone can be derived for a benchmark network by reducing its true MUL-tree to a single-copy tree,
retaining one randomly chosen instance of each taxon, and running PlaceNet on that in place of the
ASTRAL tree. This would extend the decomposition to the published topologies and account for the gap
between the μ-distance of 0.2017 reached in distribution and the 0.39 to 0.61 reached on the
benchmark.

The second direction is iterative prediction. PlaceNet predicts every event in one pass over a
single backbone, so an event nested inside another, or an event whose source clade an earlier event
has already reshaped, is representable only to the extent that the backbone already reflects it. An
iterative decode in the manner of GRAMPAIter would apply the single most confident predicted event,
re-extract the features on the resulting partial reconstruction and predict the next, terminating
when no further event is accepted. This targets the nested and overlapping events where the build
residual concentrates, and it requires a model trained on partial reconstructions rather than on
backbones alone.

The gain available to an iterative decode should be bounded before that work is undertaken. The
question is what fraction of the space of MUL-trees a single pass cannot reach at all, which can be
measured by enumerating the training networks whose event set cannot be expressed as a set of grafts
onto one backbone, independently of anything the model predicts. That fraction is the ceiling on
what iteration can recover, and it is also the part of the build residual that no amount of training
will remove.

The third direction is training data. The model was trained on six of the fifteen configurations, so
its results on the fractionated conditions and on the other effective population sizes conflate a
limit of the approach with a want of matching training data. Fractionated training data can be
generated by the same copy pruning that produced the fractionation benchmark, with no new simulation
required, and would also provide a matched split on which to calibrate the threshold of the
ploidy-free decode.

The quantity of training data has not saturated either. Allopolyploid partner accuracy rises from
0.449 at a tenth of the data to 0.828 at the full set, so generating further training networks
should raise it, and the returns are worth plotting as a curve of each measure against the number of
training networks. Computing the same curve on the true backbones of the test networks, derived by
the reduction described above, would separate what more data can give from what only a better
backbone can.

On the model itself, the propagation of the leaf features to the internal nodes is an unweighted mean
over the leaves beneath each node, which gives a taxon at the end of a long branch the same weight
as one adjacent to the node. Weighting the sum by the evolutionary distance from the node to each
leaf would make each summary local where the subtree is deep, at no cost in parameters. More
ambitiously, the hand-crafted features could be replaced by a learned encoder reading the gene trees
or the alignments directly. Phyloformer attends over pairs of sequences to predict evolutionary
distances, and an analogous encoder over pairs of gene copies could supply the placement head with a
learned measure of relatedness in place of the co-clustering fractions. Generative formulations, in
which a reconstruction is produced by a diffusion process conditioned on the evolutionary distances,
are a further possibility. An earlier learned encoder in this work did not outperform the
hand-crafted features, so either route would have to beat that baseline. The hyperparameters could
also be selected against the reconstruction measures rather than against the detection and placement
measures used here as proxies.

On the decode, the ploidy-informed mode fills each taxon greedily in order of detection confidence,
whereas an exact decode would select the event set meeting every ploidy entry exactly with the
highest total confidence, which should help on networks whose events compete for the same copies.

The diagnostic analysis bounds these changes to the model and the decode at 0.025 of μ-distance on
in-distribution networks, so each is worth pursuing together with a better backbone rather than on
its own.

Until a better backbone is available, PlaceNet is best read as a fast and robust complement to the
slower search methods rather than as a replacement for them. It recovers the polyploid lineages
that the search methods miss under discordance and fractionation, it returns an answer on every
network, and it does so in seconds.
